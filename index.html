Heritage

Traditional Clay Culture

Vietnamese pottery reflects centuries of village life, functional necessity, and artistic soul.

  • Folk Artistry: Simple clay pots, statues, and jars used in everyday household life.
  • Natural Terracotta: Fired without glaze to preserve the warm, earthy tone of raw clay.
  • Cultural Symbolism: Represents warmth, simplicity, and rural Vietnamese spirit.

Master Craftsmanship

Before firing, raw clay is meticulously shaped by hand or on traditional turntables, then arranged for air drying.

1

Manual Molding

Artisans walk backwards around stationary clay pedestals to shape large vessels seamlessly.

2

Shade Drying

Vessels dry naturally in open air to remove moisture before entering the kiln.

Process • Step 02

Terracotta Painting & Etching

Folk Motifs & Engraving

On semi-dry terracotta bodies, artisans paint or carve traditional symbols directly onto the earthenware surface.

"Every brush stroke on unbaked clay requires steady hands and deep focus."

Common themes include coconut trees, lotus flowers, rural landscapes, and mythical creatures.

Process • Step 03

Fine Porcelain Underglaze Art

Click to Enlarge

The Legacy of "Men Lam"

Famous in historic ceramic villages like Bát Tràng and Chu Đậu, underglaze cobalt painting demands extreme precision.

  • Fine Brushes: Soft bamboo brushes apply cobalt oxide pigment onto raw ceramic surfaces.
  • Chemical Transformation: Dark grey paint turns into brilliant sapphire blue upon high kiln firing.
Process • Step 04

Vibrant Glaze Formulations

Natural Mineral Glazes

Traditional Vietnamese glazes incorporate wood ash, river sand, crushed stone, and natural mineral pigments.

Floral Embossing Multi-color Glazes High-Gloss Polish

Glazed pieces are fired between 1,200°C and 1,300°C to achieve durable, glassy protective surfaces.
